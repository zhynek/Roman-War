# Yenikapı early settlement — evidence ledger

Read on 2026-10-04. **Nominal setting: circa 6000 BCE.** This falls within the
published Neolithic occupation range, without claiming its precise beginning,
a synchronous assemblage or the earliest human presence in greater Istanbul.
Yenikapı is on the historic peninsula, southwest of the later monumental center.
This is an explicitly interpretive scenario, not a recovered village plan.
There is no contemporary written description of the modeled households.

### `yenikapi_excavation` — direct archaeological evidence

Sait Başaran and Zeynep Sevim Kızıltan, “The Archaeology of the Historic Peninsula,
a World Heritage Site,” *İstanbul Special Issue* (2016), printed pp. 49–53,
PDF pp. 25–27. [Published account, accessible mirror](https://www.ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/Bosphorus-BlackSea/Yenikapi-Basaran2016.pdf).
Read the Neolithic sections, distinguished from adjacent Byzantine harbor text.
Small grouped rectangular and some round structures, timber supports, earthen
fabric and stone footing traces support the construction vocabulary. Pottery,
grinding implements and paddles anchor selected objects. Roofs, complete plans,
net racks, canoe hull, ownership and furnishing arrangements are **not** recovered
from this account. The illustrated later piers and ships are excluded.

### `algan_coast` — direct site geoarchaeology

Oya Algan et al., “Holocene coastal change in the ancient harbor of
Yenikapı–İstanbul and its impact on cultural history,” *Quaternary Research* 76
(2011), 30–45. [DOI and publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yqres.2011.04.002).
Read the indexed abstract, introduction and conclusion; full publisher retrieval
returned 403. No measured shoreline was extracted. The sequence places settlement
on a lower plain beside a stream before inundation. The model therefore uses an
independent, uncertain coastal landscape, with the sea to the south. Its stream
course, bank profile, vegetation, heights and waterline are authored. `y=0` is
local water, **not** present mean sea level or an excavated elevation.

### `ozdogan_marmara` — regional analogy

Mehmet Özdoğan, “Archaeological Evidence on the Westward Expansion of Farming
Communities from Eastern Anatolia to the Aegean and the Balkans,” *Current
Anthropology* 52, S4 (2011). [Publisher text](https://doi.org/10.1086/658895).
Read the Marmara/coastal settlement and concluding adaptation discussions.
Coastal comparisons include round/oval timber and daub forms and mixed farming,
fishing, hunting and gathering. The village's cultivation, household storage and
waterside work use that regional framework; crop species, yields, baskets, mats,
roof covering and division of labor are not established at these modeled spots.
The scene has no demographic estimate, ethnolinguistic identity or livelihood
percentages. Analogies are not proof that every household had the same equipment.

### `donmez_chronology` — site chronology and cautions

Şevket Dönmez, “The Protohistoric Times of Istanbul in the Light of New Evidence,”
*TÜBA-AR* 21 (2017), especially printed pp. 95, 98–99.
[Journal PDF](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/1726443).
Read the Neolithic discussion and stratification table. The broad 6500–5200 BCE
range accommodates the nominal date. The discussion cautions against reducing
Yenikapı's cultural identity to pottery resemblance. Its English freshwater-sea
chronology is problematic and is **not** adopted here; the geoarchaeological
study guides environmental framing. A broad settlement history is not evidence
for uninterrupted occupation of these exact plots into Byzantion or Constantinople.

## Explicit design interpretation

All nine buildings, their six dwelling uses and assigned household group IDs,
five path runs, four low screens, two cultivation plots, three working places,
landing position, light, roof pitches, openings, wear and domestic inventories
are authored. Their count measures model content only. Earthen roofs supported by
wood, reed/thatch coverings and low screens are plausible design choices, not
certified Yenikapı elevations. The small unglazed openings serve daylight and
exploration; their exact historical form is unknown. Mats and rolled bedding
avoid importing medieval beds, chests or furniture suites. The ground-level
storage arrangement is not a granary reconstruction. The hearth is outdoors.

No medieval monuments, defenses, paved roads, quays, tile roofs, chimneys, metal
forges, pottery wheels or later sailing ships are present. Burials and footprints
are acknowledged as evidence but not repurposed as scenic decoration. All geometry,
material code and object shapes are original; no source images or plans were
traced or copied. Vegetation is generic and the water is visual, not hydraulic.
