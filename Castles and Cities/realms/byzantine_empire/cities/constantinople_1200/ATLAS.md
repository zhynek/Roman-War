# District atlas and physical capabilities

This is a **spatial research brief, not a measured map**. The district names are
work-package boundaries chosen for this project; they are not asserted medieval
administrative divisions. Some packages overlap or cross the city. Stable IDs
match [study.json](study.json). Source IDs refer to [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## City framework

Establish the peninsula and its relief before placing architecture. Separate
the Golden Horn shore, Bosphorus edge and Marmara shore; connect the land-wall
system to its changing northern sector. Investigate routes between gates,
waterfronts, imperial precincts and religious centers. Preserve uncertainty in
lost street alignments and historical coastlines instead of tracing modern
roads and calling them medieval.

The first geographic deliverable is a plan with a declared projection, scale,
north direction and evidence overlay. Known anchors, inferred extents and
unresolved ground must be distinguishable. No such plan has been produced yet.

## 1. Hagia Sophia–Hippodrome precinct

**ID:** `cathedral_hippodrome` · **Priority:** first detailed focus

Study the relationship between cathedral, approaches, open spaces, Hippodrome
and palace connections. Start with plans and sections; then model a bounded
exterior precinct with its ground levels, entries and skyline. The cathedral
needs period-specific roof, buttress and interior phases rather than a modern
mesh with minarets removed.

**Capabilities to express:** religious gathering, ceremony, controlled access
and the spaces supporting those activities. These are modeling questions, not
proof of exact crowd sizes or event schedules in 1200.

**Evidence:** `met_hagia_sophia`, `met_deesis`, `uchicago_hippodrome`.
Landmark identity is supported; parcel geometry, surface condition, furnishings,
statue locations and surrounding small buildings remain unresolved. The famous
later Deesis mosaic must not appear in this reference interior.

**First artifact:** dated landmark inclusion list, source-linked footprint and
height schedule, and a neutral-light blockout once the measurements are obtained.

## 2. Great Palace and Marmara edge

**ID:** `great_palace_marmara` · **Priority:** later

Research terraces, surviving palace components, circulation and their connection
to the sea edge. Treat each building's condition and use separately. Investigate
the Boukoleon area through its own plans and chronology before modeling it.
The present evidence does not justify reconstructing every imperial room as
an intact sixth-century ensemble.

**Capabilities to express:** imperial residence and ceremony, service access,
storage and possible waterfront connections where supported.

**Evidence:** `westbrook_great_palace` (abstract only), `uchicago_hippodrome`.
The older palace continued to have uses after the court's shift toward Blachernae;
the room-by-room extent and condition around 1200 need further work.

**First artifact:** a terrace/precinct diagram with separate documented,
reconstructed and unknown extents. Do not invent a complete floor plan.

## 3. Golden Horn waterfront

**ID:** `golden_horn_waterfront` · **Priority:** next

Study individual landing places, their relation to the sea wall and public
routes, and spaces for handling and storing goods. Shore concessions and
commercial communities require chronology. Do not transplant the later Galata
tower, Genoese fortifications or a modern bridge into this scene.

**Capabilities to express:** maritime exchange, warehousing, redistribution and
provisioning. Goods require a plausible route between vessel, landing place,
storage and the street; neither throughput nor cargo mix is established yet.

**Evidence:** `schreiner_landing_stages`, `preiser_kapeller_moorings`; both
records identify limited access to the studies. Exact piers, harbor edges and
property lines remain research questions, not ready CAD dimensions.

**First artifact:** one dated waterfront transect and alternatives for uncertain
edges, followed by a small shore-access/warehouse assembly.

## 4. Blachernae palace and defenses

**ID:** `blachernae` · **Priority:** later

Research the northwestern imperial precinct, terrain, nearby defenses and
connections to the city. Separate the palace's structures from the wall system
and date individual additions. A surviving later palace building is not an
automatic reference for the 1200 residence.

**Capabilities to express:** court residence, reception, security and service
circulation. Avoid a western keep-and-bailey template without evidence.

**Evidence:** `westbrook_great_palace`, `columbia_land_walls`. Detailed palace
geometry remains unresolved. The Columbia record tentatively assigns Tekfur
Sarayı to a period after the reference year; do not include it by default.

**First artifact:** a chronology and terrain/defense relationship study before
any complete palace reconstruction.

## 5. Land-wall corridor and gates

**ID:** `land_walls` · **Priority:** next; possible component prototype

Study a specifically identified gate or wall sector, including inner and outer
defenses, moat, grade changes, access and repair phases. Develop one sourced
curtain-and-tower assembly before repeating components. Model changes of
alignment, repairs and local differences; do not distribute an idealized bay
uniformly around the perimeter.

**Capabilities to express:** fortified access, inspection and circulation.
Geometry here does not calculate siege performance or spawn a garrison.

**Evidence:** `columbia_land_walls`. The system is well identified; the exact
1200 condition and measurements of the selected sector require more detailed
documentation.

**First artifact:** one plan, elevation and section with dimension provenance,
followed by an original parametric assembly and exterior/interior inspection.

## 6. Water infrastructure transect

**ID:** `water_network` · **Priority:** next; overlaps other districts

Study elevation, incoming supply, storage and access together. Model a selected
cistern and its supported connections as an infrastructure system. Keep proposed
channels visibly distinct from known remains. Surface fountains, access points,
maintenance routes and waste handling require their own evidence.

**Capabilities to express:** water storage and distribution. An impressive
cistern mesh is not evidence of its use, filled volume or reliability in 1200.

**Evidence:** `ward_crow_crapper_water`. Its evidence spans periods and includes
uncertain routes; no complete operating network for the reference year is claimed.

**First artifact:** a sourced section showing grades and storage, with unknown
connections left open instead of completed for visual convenience.

## 7. Residential and monastic wards

**ID:** `inhabited_wards` · **Priority:** later

Build a varied ordinary neighborhood only after selecting a defensible site and
evidence. Investigate domestic and institutional compounds, open space, gardens,
street access, kitchens, storage and neighboring uses. A monastery's associated
facilities must be researched individually; do not attach an identical hospital,
school or kitchen to every religious complex.

**Capabilities to express:** dwelling, communal life and local services. Avoid
using repeated houses as a proxy census or using late Ottoman timber housing
as an unqualified Byzantine template.

**Evidence:** `ousterhout_secular`. Literary and property evidence show variety,
but do not provide a universal house plan or a measured 1200 ward.

**First artifact:** two or three explicitly labeled building-type hypotheses
and one small plot cluster, with alternatives where evidence remains weak.

## 8. Commercial routes and workshop plots

**ID:** `commercial_routes` · **Priority:** later

Research street sequences between exchange, storage, manufacture and habitation.
For any chosen craft, investigate inputs, work surfaces, fuel, water, ventilation,
stock, sale and waste. Locate noisy, dirty or fire-intensive work by evidence or
label the location as interpretive; modern zoning logic is not historical proof.

**Capabilities to express:** craft production and provisioning. These are
requirements for a believable physical model, not implemented output rates.

**Evidence:** `fhw_book_eparch`, `ousterhout_secular`,
`schreiner_landing_stages`. The tenth-century Book of the Eparch is a lead for
research questions, not a directory of shops still operating in 1200.

**First artifact:** a researched production-and-delivery sequence for one
chosen craft, followed by its plot and street section.

## Capability review

Before calling a district complete, inspect its spaces and relationships:

| Capability | What a viewer should be able to find | Questions still requiring evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Maritime exchange | Shore access, handling/storage and inland connection | Which landing place, cargoes, shore alignment and date? |
| Defense | Gate approach, wall circulation and sector differences | Which repair phases and actual 1200 fabric? |
| Administration | Public approach, controlled reception and service routes | Which palace rooms were occupied and how? |
| Religious life | Dated precinct, entries, gathering and support spaces | Which furnishings, institutions and liturgical arrangements? |
| Water | Topographically plausible supply, storage and access | What operated in 1200; which connections are inferred? |
| Craft/provisioning | Input, work, storage, sale and waste space | Which craft at which site, supported by what period evidence? |
| Domestic life | Diverse plots, entrances, yards and daily service needs | What density, construction and household arrangement is supported? |

No capability in this atlas is a gameplay unlock or an assertion of numerical
historical capacity. Later gameplay design can reference these spaces through
the existing game's reviewed data and rules.
